## Actual clash findings

Run `npx tsx scripts/auditConnectionModel.ts` for both stored demonstrations. It uses the same member, support, hardware and weld constructors as the 3D viewer; both runway rows and the reference moment-frame hardware are included. The independent report omits schematic moving crane vehicle/hub geometry. The live viewer identifies those excluded fasteners explicitly.

For the 2-ton capped example, the 24-inch tie length placed against a 20-inch column offset extends the girder-side bolt rows into the web/stiffeners. Bolt centers are at -2.5 and +0.5 inches laterally relative to the girder web. The scan identifies 240 intersecting part pairs: 120 on the primary runway and 120 on its mirrored reference. These affect 48 distinct primary-runway bolts (96 including the mirror), repeated across six girder ends. Primary pairs comprise 24 bolt-to-web, 48 bolt-to-stiffener and 48 bolt-to-stiffener-weld overlaps. This is one repeating layout problem, not 240 independent connection designs. This describes the original layout. The revised capped demonstration below replaces it; older imported projects retain the conflict warning.

The 10-ton rolled example has no detected hardware intersections in this static sample scan. This does not resolve its tieback movement verification, column-side connections or fabrication access.

## Method and limits

AABB broad-phase filtering is followed by interior radial probes through each modeled shank, head, washer and nut. Ray intersections with the actual target triangles produce signed solid intervals, including drilled holes. Positive material overlaps above 0.5 mm are reported; face contact is not a clash. Probe overlap is length along the probe, not minimum separation or physical clearance. Internal pieces of the same fastener are not compared against one another. Embedded anchor lengths and schematic wheel-hub bolts are excluded and disclosed.

This finite sample method can miss contact between probes, at hex corners, thread crests or unmodeled tolerances. A zero count is not an exact solid-Boolean or fabrication-clearance certification. Plate/member clashes away from hardware, wrench/sockets, bolt insertion, weld access, longitudinal travel, end rotation, crane swept envelopes, actual rail joints and building fabrication welds require separate review.

New weld geometry includes continuous bracket root/seat fillets, stiffener web fillets, capped top CJP surface indications, existing rolled-template flange fillets, rail-keeper roots and cap longitudinal fillets. Welds use entered sizes and lengths. CJP indication is a surface marker; preparation, internal penetration and backing are not modeled. The revised capped flange-saddle and column tie-root welds also use explicit input sizes and calculated local force paths. Reference-building fabrication welds remain outside the model.

Nine dedicated tests verify actual-hole/faying-face acceptance, undrilled shank interference, nut interference, mirror invariance, hardware-to-hardware collisions, worker-model fidelity, exclusions, fillet volume and exact-size/termination behavior. Results are saved in output/connection-review/model-clashes.json; the app provides an interactive review list, point highlighting and a JSON download. A known tie-layout warning also appears in the calculation report and export status.


## Reconfigured capped demonstration

The revised 2-ton example keeps the same three 25-ft spans, crane loads, impact, fatigue spectrum and acceptance criteria. It uses W24X94 + C15X33.9; 18-in paired ties, each 5/8 x 3-3/4 in; 5/8-in A325 bolts at 2-in pitch and 1-3/4-in gauge, with 1-in edge distances. Ties are moved 3-1/2 in from each bearing center toward their own girder end. The 12-in-long bearing plates and 4-in stiffener outstands leave the attachments within their own end regions.

Each girder tie connects through a 1-1/4-in saddle plate, 4-1/4 in along the runway, with two 5/16 fillets to the flange and two to the central gusset. Saddle width derives from the actual flange width, web thickness and a 1-in web clearance. Top bars sit 1/4 in below the cap legs; the cap and W flanges remain undrilled. Column gussets have two 5/16 root fillets. The bracket retains its 2-in seat and 1-1/2-in ribs; seat-to-rib fillets increase to 5/8 to satisfy the conservative weld-root fatigue bound.

Local calculations include elastic gusset, saddle, girder-flange and receiver-flange stress; weld-group eccentricity; local flexibility in series with tie stiffness; weld-root and base-metal fatigue; actual attachment stations in the girder fatigue register; weld size and geometric clearance limits. Elastic strip models are custom mechanics bounds, not a published/prequalified saddle design. No saddle/flange composite action or spreading beyond its actual length is credited. Full tie stress range is applied at every fatigue cycle; girder global stresses are combined with the conservative local flange range.

Revision b6c676cafa3efe3e has 601 passing rows, 7 not-applicable rows, and no failed or unresolved rows within the implemented scope. Seat fatigue damage is 0.667. Both-runway static hardware audit: 384 fasteners, 2,079 modeled solids, 1,056 candidate pairs, zero detected intersections at the unchanged 0.5-mm tolerance. These are check rows and sampled geometric pairs, not independent design validations.

S-06 and SK-11 show the actual flange attachment. S-02, S-04, the 3D model, editable dimensions and calculation report use the same geometry. This resolves the identified static clashes and modeled local resistance checks. It does **not** close longitudinal flexure/end-rotation, thermal travel, differential column shortening, global frame stresses, locating/sliding/hold-down hardware, or fabrication-tool access. The custom tieback cannot be described as a complete movement-compatible installation until those items are verified. AISC DG7 §11.2 explicitly requires this compatibility; the report retains it as a separate project-design item.
